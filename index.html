Frameworks, DPSs and dynamic markets, explained

A lot of repeat council, school and housing work isn’t advertised as a one-off tender. It’s bought through one of these three. Here’s the difference, in plain English.

Framework

A list of approved suppliers, set up for several years

A buyer, or a buying organisation acting for many buyers, picks a group of suppliers for a set period. Individual contracts (“call-offs”) are then awarded to suppliers on the list, directly or through a mini-competition.

When you can join: usually only when it’s tendered. Miss the window and you may wait years for the next one.

Dynamic purchasing system (DPS)

Like a framework, but open to new suppliers

Set up under the old rules (the Public Contracts Regulations 2015). Buyers invite suppliers on the DPS to bid for each contract. New ones are now set up as dynamic markets instead, but existing DPSs carry on until they end.

When you can join: at any time during the DPS’s life.

Dynamic market

The new version, under the Procurement Act 2023

Buyers get a list of pre-approved suppliers who have passed set checks (the “conditions of participation”). Contracts are then competed between suppliers on the market.

When you can join: at any time, and approved suppliers must be added within a reasonable time.

A place on any of these is permission to compete for work through it, not a promise of work. It can still be one of the best routes into steady public-sector contracts. Source: Government Commercial Agency.

1April 2026

New rule

Won a public contract? You may now need to be registered.

Since 1 April 2026, a supplier awarded a notifiable contract (worth £30,000 or more including VAT for councils and most other public bodies, or £12,000 or more for central government, including NHS bodies) must register on the government’s Central Digital Platform and give the buyer its unique identifier. Registration is free.

Our bid-ready pack includes doing this with you on a call, signed in under your own GOV.UK One Login.

Sources: New legislative requirements under the Procurement Act 2023, GOV.UK; How to register and find your unique identifier, GOV.UK. Wales already had a similar rule under its own regulations.

Common questions

Do I need experience of public contracts?

No. Many of the firms we speak to have never bid for public work. We’ll explain what’s involved in plain terms and help you judge whether an opportunity is realistic for your business.

What does the free suitability check involve?

You send us a specific opportunity. We review what the buyer wants, what they’re asking bidders to provide and how it fits your business, and point out the main requirements, deadlines and potential barriers, to help you decide whether the opportunity deserves a closer look. It’s an initial check, not a full assessment or a bid, and there’s no obligation.

How are fees set?

Every paid service has a fixed fee, agreed with you before any work starts. Bid packs are quoted per tender, because every tender is different. Our other services have published prices: see services and prices. Prices have no VAT added: Tendderr isn’t VAT registered. The tender alert and the initial suitability check are free.

Do you take a commission if we win?

No commission. We never take a cut of any contract you win; you pay a fixed fee for the production of your bid pack, agreed before any work starts. There are no success fees or win fees.

Do you use AI?

Yes. Our bids are AI-assisted, then reviewed and prepared by a person before anything goes to you. Calls to 0333 339 7931 are answered by a real person. Our online chat assistant is automated.

What’s the Central Digital Platform, and do I need to register?

It’s the government’s online service (part of Find a Tender) where suppliers register and get a unique identifier. Since 1 April 2026, if you win a notifiable public contract (£30,000 or more including VAT for councils and most public bodies, or £12,000 or more for central government, including NHS bodies), you must register and give the buyer your identifier. Registration is free. We can do it with you on a call as part of our bid-ready pack.

We lost a tender. Can you help us challenge it?

We can help you understand why you lost marks and plan a stronger bid next time, through our lost-bid review. We don’t give legal advice or advise on whether to challenge a decision. For that, speak to a solicitor, or raise a concern with the government’s Public Procurement Review Service.

Can you guarantee we’ll win?

No one honestly can. Buyers make the decision. What we can do is help you pick opportunities that suit you and put forward a clear, complete bid.

Do you cover Scotland or Ireland?

We cover England, Wales and Northern Ireland. Northern Ireland firms can also get alerts for, and help bidding on, Republic of Ireland public tenders on eTenders. These follow different rules (for example, tax clearance), and we’ll explain what’s involved. We don’t currently cover Scotland.
