The Procurement Act 2023: what small contractors need to know

Last checked against official sources: 30 October 2026. General information, not legal advice.

Short answer

Since 24 February 2025, most public-sector buying in England, Wales and Northern Ireland has followed the Procurement Act 2023. For small suppliers the main changes are: one free central platform (Find a Tender) for notices and supplier registration, a new ‘competitive flexible’ procedure, more open frameworks, a written assessment summary for every assessed final tender, and 30-day payment terms on a broader range of public contracts.

Who the Act covers

The government says the changes matter to anyone who supplies, or hopes to supply, goods, works or services to government departments, the NHS, local authorities, universities, schools, social housing organisations, police and fire services, and utility companies. Source: Cabinet Office short guide for suppliers.

Public procurement is devolved in Wales and Scotland and transferred in Northern Ireland. Welsh authorities follow the Act with some Welsh differences, and the Wales procurement policy statement applies instead of the national one. Devolved Scottish authorities keep using Scottish procurement law, which is why we don’t cover Scotland. Source: GOV.UK guidance on devolved contracting authorities.

What changed for suppliers

  • One free platform. Find a Tender is the central digital platform for notices and supplier registration. It’s free for everyone, and you shouldn’t pay to view public-sector opportunities.
  • A simpler, more flexible process. The Act brings in a new ‘competitive flexible’ procedure that lets buyers negotiate and work with bidders.
  • More open frameworks. The aim is that suppliers aren’t shut out of frameworks for long periods. See our frameworks, DPS and dynamic markets guide.
  • Feedback on every assessed final tender. Buyers must give bidders an assessment summary.
  • Prompt payment. 30-day payment terms apply on a broader range of public contracts, including down the supply chain.
  • Oversight. A new Procurement Review Unit includes the Public Procurement Review Service, where you can raise concerns about a specific procurement or late payment.

Source: Cabinet Office short guide for suppliers.

The thresholds from 1 January 2026

Above these values a procurement is ‘covered’ by the full rules of the Act. Below them, the lighter below-threshold rules apply. Thresholds are revised every two years.

Smaller contracts: the below-threshold rules

  • No separate pre-qualification stage. For regulated below-threshold contracts, buyers can’t use a separate suitability stage (a PQQ) to shortlist. The guidance explains this is because it can be a barrier for SMEs and VCSEs. There’s an exception for higher-value below-threshold works.
  • Notices go on Find a Tender first. Below-threshold tender notices must be published on the central digital platform.
  • Reserved competitions. Buyers may reserve below-threshold competitions for SMEs and VCSEs, or for UK or local suppliers.
  • Not every buyer. These rules don’t apply to schools or to transferred Northern Ireland authorities, unless a reserved or Welsh arrangement applies.

Source: GOV.UK guidance on below-threshold contracts.

From 1 April 2026, if you’re awarded a notifiable below-threshold contract (from £12,000 including VAT for central government and the NHS, or £30,000 for sub-central buyers such as councils), the buyer needs your unique supplier identifier from the central digital platform. Source: GOV.UK: new legislative requirements. Our registration guide explains how to get one.

Assessment summaries and the standstill period

The buyer must give an assessment summary to each supplier that submitted an assessed tender, at the same time and before it publishes the contract award notice. It explains how your tender was assessed. Source: GOV.UK guidance on assessment summaries.

Where a standstill period applies, it is at least eight working days, starting on the day the contract award notice is published. Source: GOV.UK guidance on contract award notices and standstill.

Payment and contract transparency

  • Since 1 January 2026, buyers must publish payments compliance notices showing how they’re meeting the 30-day payment terms. Each notice covers six months. Different timing applies to procurement regulated by the Welsh Ministers.
  • For contracts procured under procedures that started on or after 1 April 2026, buyers must publish payments of more than £30,000 (including VAT) every quarter.
  • On public contracts over £5 million where key performance indicators are set, buyers must publish a contract performance notice at least every 12 months.

Source: GOV.UK: new legislative requirements under the Procurement Act 2023.

Quick answers

When did the Procurement Act 2023 come into force?

On 24 February 2025. Procurements that started before then carry on under the old rules.

Does the Procurement Act 2023 apply in Scotland?

Devolved Scottish authorities continue to follow Scottish procurement law, so most Scottish public-sector buying isn’t run under the Act. Tendderr covers England, Wales and Northern Ireland, plus Irish eTenders.

Do I have to pay to see public-sector tenders?

No. Find a Tender, the central digital platform, is free for everyone. The government says you should not pay to view public-sector opportunities.

What are the procurement thresholds for 2026?

From 1 January 2026: £135,018 for central government goods and services, £207,720 for sub-central buyers such as councils, £5,193,000 for works and £663,540 for light-touch services (PPN 023).

What is an assessment summary?

It’s the written feedback that the buyer must give every supplier that submitted an assessed tender, before the contract award notice is published. It explains how your tender was scored.

How long is the standstill period?

At least eight working days, starting on the day the contract award notice is published.
