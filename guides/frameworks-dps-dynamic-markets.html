Frameworks, DPSs and dynamic markets: how to get on them

Last checked against official sources: 30 October 2026. General information, not legal advice.

Short answer

A framework is an agreement with selected suppliers that buyers then award contracts (‘call-offs’) through. Under the Procurement Act 2023 most frameworks last up to four years, and you usually can only join when one is tendered. Open frameworks reopen at least once in the first three years. A dynamic market, which replaces the dynamic purchasing system (DPS) under the Act, must accept new suppliers at any time. You can’t be charged just to be on a framework or dynamic market.

Frameworks

  • Term. Up to four years. For defence and security, and utilities frameworks, the limit is eight years. A longer term is allowed only if the nature of the work needs it, and the buyer must publish its reasons.
  • Call-offs. Buyers award contracts under a framework either by a competitive selection process (a mini-competition) or without further competition, following the framework’s terms. Contract award notices must now be published for call-offs.
  • Fees. Buyers can’t charge suppliers for access to a framework. Fees can only be charged to suppliers awarded a call-off contract, as a fixed percentage of its value.

Source: GOV.UK guidance on frameworks.

Open frameworks: a new chance to join

Open frameworks are new under the Act. They must be reopened to new suppliers at least once in the first three years, then at least every five years, up to a maximum total of eight years. When one reopens, new suppliers can join. A single-supplier open framework is limited to four years. Source: GOV.UK guidance on frameworks.

Dynamic markets (replacing the DPS)

  • The Act replaces dynamic purchasing systems and qualification systems with a single tool called a dynamic market.
  • Dynamic markets must stay open to new suppliers at any time. Buyers must consider applications within a reasonable time and admit suppliers who meet the conditions and aren’t excluded.
  • Membership conditions must be proportionate. Buyers can’t ask for audited accounts from suppliers who aren’t required to have them audited under the Companies Act 2006. They also can’t require contract insurance to be in place before award, so you can commit to having it at award.
  • For dynamic markets that aren’t utilities ones, fees can only be charged to suppliers awarded a contract, as a fixed percentage of its estimated value. They can’t be charged for membership.

Source: GOV.UK guidance on dynamic markets.

Why you still see DPSs

The Act doesn’t apply to procurements started before 24 February 2025, or to contracts awarded through frameworks or dynamic purchasing systems set up under the previous rules. So older frameworks and DPSs are still in use alongside the new tools. Source: PPN 002, paragraph 6.

Quick answers

Do I have to pay to join a public-sector framework?

Not for access. Under the Procurement Act 2023, buyers can’t charge suppliers to be on a framework. Fees can only be charged to suppliers awarded a call-off contract, as a fixed percentage of its value.

How long does a framework last?

Usually up to four years (eight for defence and security, and utilities). Open frameworks must reopen at least once in the first three years and can last up to eight years in total.

What is the difference between a DPS and a dynamic market?

The dynamic market is the Procurement Act 2023’s replacement for the dynamic purchasing system. It must accept new suppliers at any time. DPSs set up under the old rules are still in use.

Does a place on a framework guarantee work?

No. It lets you compete for, or be awarded, call-off contracts under it. It isn’t a promise of work.

Can a small firm without audited accounts join a dynamic market?

Yes, if it meets the conditions. Buyers can’t require audited accounts from suppliers that aren’t required to have them audited under the Companies Act 2006, and they must accept alternative evidence of financial capacity.
